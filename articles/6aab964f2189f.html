GMOs - ITS ECONOMIC IMPACT AND IMPLICATIONS ACROSS AFRICA

Africa is blessed with lands with rich mineral resources and arable, suited for various form of agricultural purposes. Thus, it is safe to say that Africa is well off from an economic standpoint in terms of available space to plant crop and rear animals necessary for food sustainability for the continent; but the current reality, paints a gloomy picture.

The stack reality facing most African nations is food insecurity and wide-spread hunger now compelling international food aids/agencies like the UN to feed her children. It's clear that Africa at this point needs to pay more attention towards achieving food security which in turn frees her from survival mode and invariable turn her developmental curse towards industrial revolution which ultimately will lead to food sustainability.

Achieving food sustainability milestone:

To achieve this milestone, Africa must review their land Ownership/Use acts to enable long-term ownership of arable and resource rich lands and enact guard rails preventing rogue government from waking up and taking lands as they deem fit under the guise that lands primarily belongs to the government and can be revoked when they dim fit. Land is one of the most important factor in any economy, care should be take to make its use for agricultural purposes flexible enough to encourage investors to come in with Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) funds needed to mechanise the sector and do away with the archaic subsistence farming which had held the continent down economically for decades.

We may not divide deep into the limiting factors of land acquisitions in respect to agriculture; for instance the sub-Saharan's being surrounded by deserts, making it difficult for growing cash crops or in some cases where certain regions are landlocked, thereby discouraging maritime route which is very important for trading harvested produce internationally via sea; rather we will be focusing on the remedies science and technology has provided (GMOs) for making subsistence agriculture a viable option even with these limitations.

Can the use of GMOs improve the Agricultural sector?

Politically, the use of GMOs might be frowned upon especially if it’s been provided solely by other entities. Nonetheless it does not take away the immense economic prosperity it holds. Therefore, the bond of contention should be how to acquire the technical knowhow of unlocking the potentials of genetically modified crops holds in Africa and its agricultural ecosystem. We can already see some of this progress in the livestock industry via genetic modifications of certain breeds; in this instance we will use the poultry industry as a vivid example. Through selective breeding and genetic mutations, scientists were able to breed broilers specifically for meat and layers for eggs.

    Similar examples can be sited for crops:
  • South Africa:
    Studies have shown that GM white maize in South Africa has resulted in increased yields, reduced pesticide use, and higher farm incomes.
  • Nigeria:
    GM cowpea is being developed to resist the Maruca pod borer, a major pest that significantly reduces yields.

This example portrays the economic opportunities that lie in harnessing technology to boost yields by targeting the genetics of certain crops to maximize their annual yield while being able to resist diseases or pest related losses that hamper harvest. Steps are already in progress to achieve this, there are certain varieties of maize that has been modified for high-yield, which translate to bumpy harvest for the farmers.

While we talk of the negativity of relying on foreign entities (Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation) for the supplies of these special seeds, African leaders must also consider the economic benefits of these projects, so as not to miss out on its financial boom; all because they are trying to be too carefully and only consider the negative aspect of this ground breaking innovation which is akin to: “Throwing away the baby with the bath water”.

The Push for GMOs in Agriculture

The economic implications of using GMOs in African agriculture are complex, with potential benefits being increased yields, pest control and reduction in the costs of product. As sweet as this potential sounds, watching out for: “If it's too good to be true, it's probably not” should remain the cautionary tale. Thus, farmers should be weary of the big-techs inducing influencers financially all over the media, to push for the use of GMOs without vetting its adverse effect on the agricultural chain or the economy at large.

While some studies show positive impacts in respect to the annual yields of farmer that adopted this method; and applaud Big-Techs Company for their roles towards making easy and affordable to use GMOs and its technology towards achieving food sustainability, care must be taken to overtly depending on these multinational corporations, which may led to monopoly on the long-run.

    Potential Economic Benefits:
  • Increased yields: GM crops can lead to higher yields due to traits like pest and disease resistance, potentially boosting farm incomes and food security.
  • Reduced input costs: GM crops engineered for herbicide tolerance can reduce the need for expensive herbicides, lowering production costs.
  • Improved market access: GM crops can enhance market access for farmers by producing higher quality products that meet market standards.
  • Enhanced nutritional value: Some GM crops are developed to be more nutritious, potentially addressing malnutrition and improving public health.

    Potential Economic Risks:
  • High seed costs: GM seeds can be expensive, potentially creating a financial burden for smallholder farmers.
  • Dependence on multinational corporations: Small farmers may become dependent on large seed companies for access to GM seeds and related technologies.
  • Exacerbation of inequalities: The benefits of GM crops may not be evenly distributed, potentially widening the gap between large and small farmers.
  • Intellectual property rights: Complex intellectual property rights associated with GM seeds can create barriers to access and adoption, especially for smallholders.
  • Loss of biodiversity: Monoculture farming of GM crops could lead to a decline in crop diversity and potentially impact local ecosystems.
  • Uncertainty about long-term impacts: The long-term economic and environmental consequences of GM crop cultivation are still being studied.
